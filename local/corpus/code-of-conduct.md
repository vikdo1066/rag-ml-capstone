# Acme Corp Code of Conduct

Effective date: 1 January 2026. This Code applies to employees, officers, directors, and anyone representing Acme Corp, including contractors on our premises or in our systems. It sits alongside the Leave Policy, Expense Policy, Remote Work Policy, and Data Handling Policy. Breaking this Code can lead to disciplinary action up to and including dismissal, and in some cases to reports to regulators or the police.


## 1. Purpose
Acme Corp builds software that other organisations trust with their people and operational data. Clients, colleagues, and the public should be able to assume that we tell the truth in our work, treat people with respect, protect information, and do not use our position for improper personal gain.


## 2. Respect at work
Harassment, bullying, discrimination, and retaliation are prohibited. This includes conduct based on race, ethnicity, nationality, sex, gender identity, sexual orientation, religion, disability, age, pregnancy, or union membership, and it includes conduct that happens on video calls, in chat tools, at offsites, and on social media when the person is identifiable as an Acme employee. A single severe incident can be enough. Jokes, nicknames, and “banter” are judged by impact, not intent.

Romantic or sexual relationships between a manager and someone in their reporting line, or between anyone who can affect the other’s pay, performance rating, or staffing, must be disclosed to People Operations. Failure to disclose is itself a breach.


## 3. Integrity of work and communications
Do not fabricate metrics, demo data presented as production, or client references. Do not hide outages or security incidents from the incident channel. Marketing and sales claims must be supportable. Internal messages are not a licence to be cruel; they may be disclosed in an investigation.

Use of company systems is monitored to the extent permitted by law. There is no expectation of privacy on company laptops, email, or chat, except where local law creates one.


## 4. Conflicts, gifts, and bribery
A conflict exists when personal interests could reasonably be seen to affect a company decision. Examples: hiring a relative, steering a contract to a firm you own shares in, or using a vendor who employs your partner. Disclose conflicts in writing to Legal before the decision is made.

Do not offer, promise, or accept bribes, kickbacks, or facilitation payments, including through third parties. Gifts and hospitality must be modest, occasional, and recorded if over £25. Cash or cash equivalents are never acceptable. Public-sector clients have stricter rules; follow the client’s policy if it is tighter than ours.


## 5. Confidentiality and assets
Client names, unpublished product plans, credentials, and non-public financials are confidential. Do not post them in public GitHub issues, screenshots on social media, or unapproved AI tools. Company devices and source code remain company property. On leaving, return equipment and revoke personal copies of repositories.

Personal use of tools is allowed in a limited way (for example a short personal email) but cryptocurrency mining, unlicensed software, and storing large personal media libraries on company machines are forbidden.


## 6. Speaking up
Report concerns to your manager, to People Operations, to legal@acme.example, or through the anonymous helpline published on the intranet. Reports of discrimination, safety issues, or suspected crime can skip the management chain. Acme Corp prohibits retaliation against anyone who reports in good faith or participates in an investigation. Knowingly false reports are a breach of this Code.

Managers who ignore a report, or who try to identify an anonymous reporter in order to punish them, will themselves be investigated.


## 7. Outside activities
Paid outside work needs written approval if it overlaps with Acme’s market, uses company time or tools, or exceeds eight hours a week. Board seats and advisory roles must be disclosed. Political activity must be personal: do not use company letterhead, logos, or mailing lists to campaign.

This Code does not replace the law. If it conflicts with a mandatory legal right, the legal right prevails. People Operations owns this document and reviews it annually.
